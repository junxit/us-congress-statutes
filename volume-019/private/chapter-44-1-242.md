# Chapter 242

> An Act For the relief of Janies F. Buckner.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That the proper accounting officers of the Treasury Department be, and they are hereby, authorized and directed, in the settlement of the accounts of James F. Buckner, collector of internal revenue for the fifth district of Kentucky, to credit and allow such amounts as are shown to have been stolen or embezzled by his late deputy collector and cashier, George N. Jackson, it being first proved to the satisfaction of the Secretary of the Treasury that such embezzlement or larceny did not occur through any fault or negli-gence of said Buckner: Provided, That the said Buckner shall assign and transfer to the United States, and for their benefit, any bond or other security, lien by judgment, or otherwise, against the said Jackson or others, and the right to prosecute all suit or suits thereon or for the enforcement thereof, and the exclusive benefit of all such shall inure to the United States.


> MILTON SAYLER,Speaker of the House of Representatives pro tempore.
> T. W. FERRYPresident of the Senate pro tempore


*Received by the President July 19, 1876.*
