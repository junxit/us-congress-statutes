# Chapter 535

> AN ACT For the relief of Charles G. Johnson, State treasurer of the State of California.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That the Secretary of the Treasury is authorized and directed to pay, out of any money in the Treasury not otherwise appropriated, to Charles G. Johnson, State treasurer of the State of California, the sum of $17,500. Such sum represents the value of ten coupons from 3½ per centum Treasury notes, series C–1930–32, numbered 3512B, 3513C, 3514D, 4361A, 4362B, 4363C, 4364D, 4365E, 4366F, and 4367H, of the $100,000 denomination, which coupons were payable on December 15, 1929, and were lost or destroyed in the office of the State treasurer of California: Provided, That none of said coupons shall have been presented to the Treasury for payment and that Charles G. Johnson shall first file in the Treasury Department a bond in the penal sum of double the amount of the sum payable pursuant to the provisions of this Act, in such form and with such corporate surety as may be acceptable to the Secretary of the Treasury, to indemnify and save harmless the United States from any loss on account of such lost or destroyed coupons.


*Approved, June 5, 1936.*
