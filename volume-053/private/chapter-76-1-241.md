# Chapter 241

> AN ACT For the relief of George Houston.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That the limitations of time in sections 15 to 20, both inclusive, of the Act entitled “An Act to provide compensation for employees of the United States suffering injuries while in the performance of their duties, and for other purposes”, approved September 7, 1916, as amended (U. S. C., 1934 ed., title 5, secs. 767 and 770), are hereby waived in favor of George Houston, who is alleged to have sustained an injury on February 4, 1931, while employed as a rural mail carrier from the post office at Wood Lake, Minnesota, which resulted in permanent physical disability, and his case is authorized to be considered and acted upon under the remaining provisions of such Act, as amended, if he files a claim for compensation with the United States Employees’ Compensation Commission not later than six months after the date of enactment of this Act: Provided, That no benefits shall accrue prior to the approval of this Act.


*Approved, June 22, 1939.*
