# Chapter 344

> AN ACT For the relief of Sadako Takagi.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That the provisions of the immigration laws relating to the exclusion of aliens inadmissible because of race shall not hereafter apply to Sadako Takagi, the Japanese fiancee of Lieutenant William M. Marutani, of Chicago, Illinois, presently a tubercular patient at the Veterans’ Administration Hospital in Waukesha, Wisconsin, and a retired United States Army officer of World War II, and that Sadako Takagi may be eligible for a visa as a nonimmigrant temporary visitor for a period of three months: Provided, That the administrative authorities find that the said Sadako Takagi is coming to the United States with a bona fide intention of being married to said Lieutenant William M. Marutani, and that she is found otherwise admissible under the immigration laws. In the event that the marriage between the above-named parties does not occur within three months after the entry of said Sadako Takagi, she shall be required to depart from the United States and upon failure to do so shall be deported in accordance with the provisions of sections 19 and 20 of the Immigration Act of February 5, 1917 (U. S. C., title 8, sections 155 and 156). In the event the marriage between the above-named parties shall occur within three months after the entry of said Sadako Takagi, the Attorney General is authorized and directed to record the lawful admission for permanent residence of said Sadako Takagi as of the date of her entry into the United States, upon the payment by her of the required fees and head tax.


*Approved July 18, 1949.*
