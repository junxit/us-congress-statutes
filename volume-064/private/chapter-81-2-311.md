# Chapter 311

> AN ACT For the relief of Hisako Nakane.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and Bouse of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That the provisions of the immigration laws relating to the exclusion of aliens inadmissible because of race shall not hereafter apply to Hisako Nakane, Kyote, Japan, the Japanese fiancée of Norman L. Compton, a citizen of the United States and an honorably discharged veteran of World War II, and that Hisako Nakane may be eligible for a visa as a nonimmigrant temporary visitor for a period of three months: Provided, That the administrative authorities find that the said Hisako Nakane is coming to the United States with a bona fide intention of being married to said Norman L. Compton, and that she is found otherwise admissible under the immigration laws. In the event the marriage between the above-named parties does not occur within three months after the entry of said Hisako Nakane, she shall be required to depart from the United States and upon failure to do so shall be deported in accordance with the provisions of sections 19 and 20 of the Immigration Act of February 5, 1917 (U. S. C., title 8, secs. 155 and 156). In the event the marriage between the above-named parties shall occur within three months after the entry of said Hisako Nakane, the Attorney General of the United States is authorized and directed to record the lawful admission for permanent residence of said Hisako Nakane as of the date of her entry into the United States, upon the payment by her of the required fees and head taxes.


*Approved June 17, 1950.*
