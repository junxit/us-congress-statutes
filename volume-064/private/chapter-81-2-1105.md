# Chapter 1105

> AN ACT For the relief of Hifumi Kato and her minor son, Kazuyuki Kato.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That the provisions of the immigration laws relating to the exclusion of aliens inadmissible because of race shall not hereafter apply to Hifumi Kato, the Japanese fiancée of John B. Howenstein, a citizen of the United States and an honorably discharged veteran of World War II, and her son, Kazuyuki Kato, and that said Hi funri Kato and her above-named son may be eligible for visas as nonimmigrant temporary visitors for a period of three months: Provided, That the administrative authorities find that the said Hifumi Kato is coming to the United States with a bona fide intention of being married to said John B. Howenstein, and that they are found otherwise admissible under the immigration laws. In the event the marriage between the above-named parties does not occur within three months after the entry of said Hifumi Kato and her son, they shall be required to depart from the United States and upon failure to do so shall be deported in accordance with the provisions of sections 19 and 20 of the Immigration Act of February 5, 1917 (U. S. C., title 8, sections 155 and 156). In the event the marriage between the above-named parties shall occur within three months after the entry of Hifumi Kato and her son, the Attorney General is authorized and directed to record the lawful admission for permanent residence of them as of the date of their entry into the United States, upon the payment by them of the required visa fees and head taxes.


*Approved September 28, 1950.*
