# Chapter 798

> AN ACT For the relief of Hisako Okamoto.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That the provisions of the immigration laws relating to the exclusion of aliens inadmissible because of race shall not hereafter apply to Hisako Okamoto, the Japanese fiancée of Frank Koshak, a citizen of the United States and an honorably discharged veteran of World War II, and that Hisako Okamoto may be eligible for a visa as a nonimmigrant temporary visitor for a period of three months: Provided, That the administrative authorities find that the said Hisako Okamoto is coming to the United States with a bona fide intention of being married to said Frank Koshak, and that she is found otherwise admissible under the immigration laws. In the event the marriage between the above-named parties does not occur within three months after entry of said Hisako Okamoto, she shall be required to depart from the United States and upon failure to do so shall be deported in accordance with the provisions of sections 19 and 20 of the Immigration Act of February 5, 1917 (U. S. C., title 8, secs. 155 and 156). In the event the marriage between the above-named parties shall occur within three months after entry of said Hisako Okamoto, the Attorney General is authorized and directed to record the lawful admission for permanent residence of said Hisako Okamoto as of the date of her entry into the United States, upon the payment by her of the required fees and head taxes.


*Approved August 25, 1950.*
