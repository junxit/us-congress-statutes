# Chapter 1208

> AN ACT For the relief of Tomoko Yamaya.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That the provisions of the immigration laws relating to the exclusion of aliens inadmissible because of race shall not hereafter apply to Tomoko Yamaya, the Japanese fiancée of Paul H. Vine, a citizen of the United States and an honorably discharged veteran of World War II, and that the said Tomoko Yamaya may be eligible for a visa as a nonimmigrant temporary visitor for a period of three months: Provided, That the administrative authorities find that the said Tomoko Yamaya is coming to the United States with a bona fide intention of being married to Paul H. Vine, and that she is found otherwise admissible under the immigration laws. In the event the marriage between the above-named Îarties does not occur within three months after the entry of the said Tomoko Yamaya, she shall required to depart from the United States and upon failure to do so shall be deported in accordance with the provisions of sections 19 and 20 of the Immigration Act of February 5, 1917 (8 U. S. C. 155, 156). In the event the marriage between the above-named parties shall occur within three months after the entry of the said Tomoko Yamaya, the Attorney General is authorized and directed to record the lawful admission for permanent residence of the said Tomoko Yamaya as of the date of her entry into the United States, upon payment of the required head tax and visa fee.


*Approved January 3, 1951.*
