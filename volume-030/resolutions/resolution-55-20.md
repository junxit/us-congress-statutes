# Resolution 20

> Joint Resolution Grunting permission for the erection of a monument or statue in Washington City, District of Columbia, in honor of the late Albert Pike.

*Resolved by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That permission be, and is hereby, granted the Supreme Council of the Inspectors-General of the Thirty-Third Degree of the Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite of Freemasonry for the Southern Jurisdiction of the United States of America to erect a monument or statue to the memory and in honor of Albert Pike, in such place in the city of Washington, District of Columbia, other than the Capitol or Library grounds, as shall be designated by the Superintendent of Public Buildings and Grounds, the Joint Committee on the Library, and the committee of the Supreme Council of said rite appointed by it for that purpose. The said monument or statue shall not cost less than ten thousand dollars, and to be presented to the people of the United States by said Supreme Council.


*Approved, April 9, 1898.*
