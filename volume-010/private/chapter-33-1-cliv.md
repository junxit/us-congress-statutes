# Chapter CLIV

> An Act confirming a certain Land Claim in Louisiana known as the Fleuriau Claim.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That the heirs, assigns, and legal representatives of Charles J. B. Fleuriau, or Floriau, be, and they are hereby, confirmed in their claim to a tract of land described in a petition or request addressed by Joseph Villars Dubreuil to the Governor and Commissary of Marine of the Province of Louisiana, on the first day of June, seventeen hundred and sixty-three, as the same was surveyed by A. F. Righter, a deputy-surveyor, in the year eighteen hundred and thirty-nine, and certified by H. T. Williams, Surveyor-General of the State of Louisiana, on the fourteenth of December, eighteen hundred and thirty-nine, and for the full extent of the land embraced in said sur- veys; and that a patent shall issue therefor: Provided, That this act shall be held and taken only as a relinquishment on the part of the United States.


*Approved, July 27, 1854.*
