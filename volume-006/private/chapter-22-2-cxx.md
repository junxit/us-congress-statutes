# Chapter CXX

> An Act for the relief of James Range, a soldier of the Revolution.

*Be it enacted, &c.,*

That it shall be the duty of the Secretary of the Treasury to issue to James Range, upon his surrendering to the Commissioner of the General Land Office a duplicate of a warrant for one hundred acres, number six thousand three hundred and fifty-four, (the original of which being lost,) issued to him upon [the] twenty-first of January, one thousand eight hundred and twenty, by the state of Virginia, for one hundred acres of land, due said Range, in consideration of three years’ services as a private in the continental line, land scrip at the rate of one dollar and twenty-five cents per acre, in the same form as though the original of said warrant was surrendered; which scrip shall be received in payment for any lands subject to private entry in either of the states of Ohio, Indiana, or Illinois.


*Approved, March 2, 1833.*
