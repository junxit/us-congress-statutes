# Public Law 103–251

> Joint Resolution Designating May 1, 1994, through May 7, 1994, as “National Walking Week”.

*Resolved by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

Whereas medical authorities have established that walking— Whereas the failure to exercise regularly, such as walking, has been identified as the single greatest risk factor for heart disease; Whereas the designation of “National Walking Week” will help promote the issue of pedestrian access and safety; Whereas areas of America are becoming inaccessible or unsafe for walkers, so the benefits of this activity are being blocked; Whereas people should be able to walk anywhere in their community, within reason; and Whereas walking encourages community spirit and safety: Now, therefore, be it

- **(1)** powerfully protects against high blood pressure, cholesterol problems, and other factors that can contribute to heart disease;
- **(2)** protects against adult onset (Type II) diabetes;
- **(3)** builds strong bones and protects against osteoporosis, the weak-bone disease that afflicts millions of older women;
- **(4)** probably offers protection against several forms of cancer that are believed to be preventable through regular and moderate exercise; and
- **(5)** is a safe and dependable way for millions of overweight people to lose weight without stringent dieting;

That May 1, 1994, through May 7, 1994, is designated as “National Walking Week”. The President is authorized and requested to issue a proclamation calling upon the people of the United States to observe the week with appropriate ceremonies and activities.


*Approved May 16, 1994.*
