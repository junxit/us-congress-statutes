# Private Law 95–150

> An Act For the relief of John F. Johnson.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That John F. Johnson, a postmaster at the United States Post Office in Beech Grove, Indiana, is relieved of liability to the United States in the amount of $767.98, representing losses resulting from the mailing at an improper rate during the period January 10, 1966, through June 30, 1968, of the Perry Township Weekly, a publication of Perry Publications, Incorporated, a corporation incorporated in the State of Indiana. In the audit and settlement of the accounts of any certifying or disbursing officer of the United States, full credit shall be given for the amount for which liability is relieved by this Act.

## § 2.

- **(a)** The Secretary of the Treasury shall pay, out of any money in the Treasury not otherwise appropriated, to John F. Johnson an amount equal to the aggregate of any amounts paid by him to, or withheld from sums otherwise due him by, the United States with respect to his indebtedness to the United States referred to in the first section of this Act.
- **(b)** Not more than 10 per centum of the amount appropriated in subsection (a) of this section may be transferred, directly or indirectly, to any attorney or other agent as consideration for service rendered to John F. Johnson in connection with this claim. Any person violating the provisions of this subsection shall be fined not more than $1,000.


*Approved October 31, 1978.*
