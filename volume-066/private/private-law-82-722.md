# Private Law 722

> AN ACT For the relief of Else Neubert and her two children.

*Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,*

That, in the administration of the immigration and naturalization laws. Else Neubert, the German fiancée of Sergeant Clyde E. Fritz, a United States citizen now serving in the United States Army, and her two children shall be eligible for visas as nonimmigrant temporary visitors for a period of three months: Provided, That the administrative authorities find that the said Else Neubert is coming to the United States with a bona fide intention of being married to the said Sergeant Fritz, and that she and her two children are found otherwise admissible under the immigration laws. In the event the marriage between the above-named parties does not occur within three months after the entry of the said Else Neubert, she and her two children shall be required to depart from the United States, and upon failure to do so shall be deported in accordance with the provisions of sections 19 and 20 of the Immigration Act of 1917, as amended (U.S. C., title 8, secs. 155 and 156). In the event that the marriage between the above-named parties shall occur within three mouths after the entry of the said Else Neubert, the Attorney General is authorized and directed to record the lawful admission for permanent residence of the said Else Neubert and her two children as of the date of the payment of the required visa fees and head taxes.


*Approved June 27, 1952.*
